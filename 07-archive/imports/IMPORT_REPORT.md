## Contradictions and unresolved variants

| Issue | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- |
| Valve terminology | The early beat calls Lena's chosen catalyst-consuming action a bypass. The later materials distinguish a tested, catalyst-consuming action from an untaken, untested cold bypass. |
| Inevitability | The author initially asks for an outcome that feels inevitable regardless of what the characters try. The later notes leave one uncertain counterfactual. How to preserve both effects remains an author decision. |
| Resource accounting | The early premise ties the resource loss and access to the solution to an earlier refusal to sacrifice systems or data. The later outline separates the first catalyst burn, subsequent costs, and preservation of the stream. Which choices exhaust the gel and crystals remains unspecified. |
| Lena's fate and the third beat | The early diagram has “Lena After” and explicitly includes her suicide after the Second Call. The handoff reopens her fate, and the cinematic diagram substitutes “The Cost” and leaves the second cost unspecified. These versions do not establish a single sequence. |
| Chapter breaks and arrows | Early diagrams disagree about whether the Valve sits inside Chapter 1 and differ in how the Second Call, Pattern, Inventory, and Blister connect. The later chart is a continuous seven-beat sequence without chapter boundaries. |
| Earth and the synopsis | The timing and mechanism of final Earth failure are unresolved relative to earlier descriptions of an uninhabitable Earth. Separately, the internal blister frame and the archived teaser depict Earth dying. Review where public disclosure should stop under the later spoiler-free constraint. |
