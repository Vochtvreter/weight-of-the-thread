### Stay Until Morning

When the alarm went off in auxiliary control, I knew the sequence. I could see the first control my hand was supposed to go to. I stood there looking at it until the alarm cleared.

I hadn't even been hurt. By the time I got to the coolant exchange, I'd gone through the sequence in my head so many times that it felt like something I'd actually done. Then I'd remember my hand hanging by my side.

I sat in the recess beside the exchange, where the corridor lights stopped short of the wall. The floor felt cold through my trousers. There was a hum down there I couldn't hear standing up. I could feel it through both heels if I planted them flat.

Marcus came around the corner carrying two cups. When he saw me, he stopped long enough to shift both cups into one hand. He put the other against the wall and lowered himself onto the floor.

“Here,” he said.

It was the imitation coffee. It had that scorched smell that was closer to coffee than the taste ever got. The reusable cup was warm enough that I put both hands around it.

Marcus took a sip of his. “I got the one with the lid that leaks again.”

I looked down. There was a wet spot on his cuff. He rubbed it with his thumb, which made it bigger.

We drank for a while. A vibration rose under the floor, held for a few seconds, then dropped back to the hum.

“I knew what to do,” I said.

He set his cup between his boots.

“I didn't do anything. I just stood there.” I rubbed at the seam on the cup with my thumbnail. “People are going to expect me to get it right because I usually do. They might not have time to notice I'm just standing there.”

Marcus waited. I wished he'd say that knowing the procedure counted for something. I could have agreed with that and finished my coffee.

“What if it happens when it matters?”

“It probably will.”

I looked at him.

“You'll get scared again,” he said. “You'll be unsure. You'll get things wrong. I still do.”

“Were you afraid during the war?”

“Yes.” He picked at the wet cloth on his wrist. “I made mistakes, too. Some of them I knew were mistakes as soon as I'd made them. I was still scared the next time.”

I waited for him to tell me which ones. He reached for his cup, and I let him.

Across the corridor the lights dimmed to their night setting. I could still see his boots, and the stripe of light at the bottom of the wall.

“What if I can't do it?”

“Then we'll find someone who can.”

“And if there isn't anyone?”

He leaned his head back against the wall. “Then we'll be frightened together.”

I looked at the lid of my cup. There was a little ridge where the plastic had warped. I'd kept my thumb on it the whole time.

“I don't think I'm the person they need,” I said.

“You don't have to become the person everyone needs you to be tonight.”

I tried to take a drink. The lid clicked against my teeth.

“Elias,” he said. “You can ask me for help. Even after you've got something wrong. I'd still have come looking for you.”

He put his hand on the back of my neck. His palm was warm, and his thumb caught for a second in the collar of my shirt. I bent my head. I managed one breath before I started to cry.

He took the cup out of my hands and set it on the floor. I pressed my sleeve to my face. His hand stayed where it was.

Later, when I could talk again, I said, “You've got early shift.”

“I know.”

“You should sleep.”

He stretched one leg out into the corridor. His knee made a small click. He left the other bent so I could lean against him without sliding down.

I meant to sit up once I'd caught my breath. The floor was hard enough that I'd lost the feeling in one heel. I shifted it, found a place for my head against his shoulder, and listened to the machinery until I fell asleep.

I woke when the light along the wall began to come up. Both cups were cold on the floor. Marcus was still there, his shoulder warm under my cheek.
