# Visual style and character prompts

## Shared visual style

Use matte gouache on lightly textured paper, naturalistic anatomy, simplified shapes, dry-brush edges, and restrained paper grain. Build the palette from charcoal, slate blue, and warm bone, with one subdued accent per subject. Light scenes with broad, practical sources and cooler reflected fill. Give station surfaces and clothing the marks of ordinary use. For environments and objects, use the same paint handling, palette, and lighting with framing appropriate to the subject. For diagrams and maps, keep labels and structure legible and use the palette for emphasis.

## Generating new images

Each current prompt contains the complete text used for its matching portrait. Use the shared portrait block below as a starting point when drafting a new prompt. For a scene, replace the portrait framing and background with the scene’s composition and action. Preserve the character’s apparent age, facial structure, hair, and signature clothing details unless the scene calls for a specific change. Attach the full-resolution reference when the generator accepts images. For Ava, keep the same face across outfits and settings, and preserve the limited projection effects and lack of physical contact. For group scenes, identify each character and their position in the frame before adding the character blocks.

### Shared portrait block

> Create one vertical 2:3 character concept portrait for The Weight of the Thread, a grounded orbital science-fiction tragedy. Hand-painted matte gouache on lightly textured paper, with naturalistic anatomy, simplified shapes, visible dry-brush edges, and fine paper grain. Muted charcoal, slate blue, warm bone, and a single subdued costume accent. Light the subject with a broad, warm work light from camera left and a cooler reflected fill. Show a chest-up three-quarter view at eye level, face near the upper third, with space around the head and shoulders for cropping. Use a softly painted habitat bulkhead with one or two panel seams. Station textiles look practical and repeatedly laundered. Keep expressions small and legible. Use a single figure, without lettering, emblems, neon, heroic poses, or glossy 3D rendering.
