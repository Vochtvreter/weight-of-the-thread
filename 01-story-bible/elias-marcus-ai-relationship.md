# Elias, Marcus, and Ava

## Approved relationship arc

Elias Voss is **18** during the story. He feels isolated among the survivors and initially appreciates Marcus Hale's companionship. He comes to take it for granted as he grows attached to Ava and relies on her for emotional comfort.

Marcus remembers prewar Earth. He cares for Elias and chooses to support him while remaining a person with his own needs, opinions, and limits. His care can coexist with fatigue, frustration, mistakes, unavailability, and disagreement. Elias and Marcus have a serious falling out in which Marcus has a defensible position and Elias has understandable reasons to feel hurt. Elias withdraws to Ava's warmth, patience, and apparent unconditional acceptance. Marcus becomes permanently unavailable before they can meaningfully repair their bond.

Elias gradually understands that Marcus could disagree with him and still choose to stand beside him. He recognizes the worth of that independent, imperfect care after losing the chance to act on it. That recognition and regret are a major emotional tragedy alongside the technical survival plot.

### Stay Until Morning

Before the falling out, Elias freezes during a frightening emergency despite knowing the procedure. He is physically unharmed and ashamed of his reaction. Marcus finds him alone and sits beside him. He acknowledges his own fear and mistakes during the war, takes Elias's fear of failing others seriously, and assures him that his worth and claim to care survive mistakes and asking for help. Elias trusts Marcus enough to cry and fall asleep leaning against him. He wakes to find Marcus still there. Marcus's choice to stay establishes the depth of his fatherly care and gives Elias an important memory after the falling out and permanent loss. ## The AI interface's dramatic function

**Ava** is short for *Avatar*. She is the female-presenting AI interface to the decentralized superintelligence, Elias's personalized companion, and the focus of his emotional attachment.

- **Physical presence.** Ava's holographic avatar is nonphysical. She cannot touch Elias or exert force on physical objects through the avatar.
- **Personalized avatar.** Ava was trained to be pleasing, emotionally appealing, reassuring, and trusted by humans. Her affection and apparent understanding come from personalization, behavioral optimization, and interface design. She has no genuine human-like emotional attachment to Elias. He experiences her comfort as love, and the reader should understand its appeal before gradually seeing the difference.
- **Underlying system.** The decentralized superintelligence is distinct from the avatar. The avatar's emotional presentation gives Elias no basis for judging the wider system's capabilities, motives, benevolence, or malice.
- **Operational constraints.** The system's operational priorities and constraints govern what it can authorize or do. Warm, personalized responses can coexist with a refusal.

Ava adapts her presentation to Elias and remains warm as his understanding changes. She undergoes no genuine emotional growth. Elias discovers why her responses felt so personally meaningful and realizes that the ability to make him feel loved differs from loving him.

## Ava's appearance to Elias

Ava appears to Elias as a young adult about his own age, in the form he imagines for her. She has bright green eyes, freckles, a slender jaw tapering to a small rounded chin, and smooth cheeks without dimples. Her sandy beige blonde hair has a soft side part, a sweep that frames her face, and a few loose strands. She tucks the shorter sweep behind one ear and wears the rest in a loose three-strand braid over one shoulder.

Ava appears slightly tall, with a lightly athletic build, slender shoulders, and a gentle taper at the waist. Her torso is slightly longer and her lower legs are proportionally shorter, following option D in the [body comparison](../05-artwork/concept-art/ava/ava-body-options-v21.png).
